General Insights

Slow Week on Stock Market

Global equity markets faced a sharp downturn this week as uncertainty over central bank policies and macroeconomic conditions rattled investor sentiment, with the MSCI gauge of global stocks posting its largest weekly drop in two months, sliding 1.0%. U.S. Treasury yields climbed to six-month highs, with the 10-year yield reaching 4.51% after Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell signaled no urgency for rate cuts. In the U.S., major indices saw significant losses; the Dow Jones dropped 1.24%, the S&P 500 fell 2.08%, and the Nasdaq Composite plunged 3.15%, marking its worst week since January as tech giants like Nvidia, Apple, and Microsoft collectively lost $458 billion in market value. Commodities also took a hit, with gold prices slumping 5% to $2,536 per ounce, their worst week since March, and Brent crude ending at $71.04 per barrel amid weak demand forecasts from China. Indian markets mirrored global trends, with the Nifty50 and Sensex dropping 2.5% and 2.39%, respectively, led by declines in metals, FMCG, and PSU banks, while IT stocks gained on strength in Infosys and HCL Tech. The rupee hit a record low of 84.41 against the dollar, exacerbating concerns over FII outflows of ₹9,683.64 crore. Looking ahead, volatility is expected to persist as markets digest geopolitical challenges, Nvidia’s earnings, global PMI data, and China’s economic signals, while in India, the NTPC Green Energy IPO and Maharashtra elections may influence sentiment. On the technical front, Nifty is near critical support at 23,500-23,480, with 23,250 as the next support if breached, and resistance at 23,850 on recovery.
